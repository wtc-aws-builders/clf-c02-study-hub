# CapEx vs OpEx

- Domain: D1
- Task statement: 1.4
- Author: @ZukoG

## What it is

CapEx (capital expenditure) is paying a large amount up front for things you own, like servers and a data centre. OpEx (operational expenditure) is paying as you go for what you use. Moving to AWS swaps most CapEx for OpEx.

## When to use it

Any time an exam question talks about avoiding big upfront hardware costs, paying only for what you use, or turning fixed costs into variable costs.

## Pricing model

With AWS you pay per use (per hour, per GB or per request) with no upfront commitment, which is OpEx. Reserved Instances and Savings Plans bring back a small upfront commitment in exchange for a lower price.

## Easily confused with

Economies of scale. That is about AWS getting lower prices because of its size and passing them on, not about whether you pay upfront or as you go.

## Exam-style question

A start-up does not want to spend money on servers before it knows how many customers it will have. Which benefit of the AWS Cloud does this describe?

- A) Trade fixed expense for variable expense
- B) Benefit from massive economies of scale
- C) Go global in minutes
- D) Increase speed and agility

Answer: A

Why: Paying only for the servers you use instead of buying them upfront is trading fixed (capital) expense for variable expense. Economies of scale is about lower prices, not when you pay.
